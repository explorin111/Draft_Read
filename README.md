## 3. Official syllabus (verbatim, GATE 2027 = GATE 2026, unchanged)

### Probability and Statistics
Counting (permutation and combinations), probability axioms, sample space, events, independent events, mutually exclusive events, marginal, conditional and joint probability, Bayes Theorem, conditional expectation and variance, mean, median, mode and standard deviation, correlation, and covariance, random variables, discrete random variables and probability mass functions, uniform, Bernoulli, binomial distribution, continuous random variables and probability distribution function, uniform, exponential, Poisson, normal, standard normal, t-distribution, chi-squared distributions, cumulative distribution function, conditional PDF, Central limit theorem, confidence interval, z-test, t-test, chi-squared test.

### Linear Algebra
Vector space, subspaces, linear dependence and independence of vectors, matrices, projection matrix, orthogonal matrix, idempotent matrix, partition matrix and their properties, quadratic forms, systems of linear equations and solutions; Gaussian elimination, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, determinant, rank, nullity, projections, LU decomposition, singular value decomposition.

### Calculus and Optimization
Functions of a single variable, limit, continuity and differentiability, Taylor series, maxima and minima, optimization involving a single variable.

### Programming, Data Structures and Algorithms
Programming in **Python**, basic data structures: stacks, queues, linked lists, trees, hash tables; search algorithms: linear search and binary search; basic sorting algorithms: selection sort, bubble sort and insertion sort; divide and conquer: mergesort, quicksort; introduction to graph theory; basic graph algorithms: traversals and shortest path.

### Database Management and Warehousing
ER-model, relational model: relational algebra, tuple calculus, SQL, integrity constraints, normal form, file organization, indexing, data types, data transformation such as normalization, discretization, sampling, compression; data warehouse modelling: schema for multidimensional data models, concept hierarchies, measures: categorization and computations.

### Machine Learning
**(i) Supervised:** regression and classification problems, simple linear regression, multiple linear regression, ridge regression, logistic regression, k-nearest neighbour, naive Bayes classifier, linear discriminant analysis, support vector machine, decision trees, bias-variance trade-off, cross-validation methods (LOO, k-fold), multi-layer perceptron, feed-forward neural network.
**(ii) Unsupervised:** clustering algorithms, k-means/k-medoid, hierarchical clustering, top-down, bottom-up: single-linkage, multiple-linkage, dimensionality reduction, principal component analysis.

### AI
Search: informed, uninformed, adversarial; logic: propositional, predicate; reasoning under uncertainty — conditional independence representation, exact inference through variable elimination, approximate inference through sampling.

**Run DBMS in parallel now** (~60% LA / 40% DBMS). It is the only subject with zero prerequisites.

### On Gilbert Strang

Right content fit — 18.06 maps almost topic-for-topic onto the LA syllabus. But LA averages **11.7 marks** and 18.06 is a full MIT semester (~34 lectures). **Time-box to 4–5 weeks.** Do not finish the course for its own sake.

Failure mode: three months of beautifully-understood linear algebra, arriving at February having never opened DBMS — which just paid 18 marks.

Strang under-covers: **idempotent matrices, partition/block matrices, quadratic forms.** Patch from a GATE-oriented source.

**Using the book for questions — role split:**

- **Strang's book problem sets = learning and thinking**, not a GATE question bank. His problems are conceptual/geometric and slow; GATE LA is fast computation plus property recall ("rank of this 4x4 matrix", "eigenvalues of an idempotent matrix"). Different skills.
- The **Challenge Problems** at the end of each problem set are the genuinely interesting ones — that is where the brainstorming value sits.
- **[MIT OCW 18.06 (Spring 2010)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/pages/assignments)** assigns problems *from the book* and publishes **problem sets AND exams with full solutions, free**. Better than the book alone because you get graded-style questions with worked answers. [18.06SC (Fall 2011)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/) adds problem-solving videos on every topic.
- **Edition:** if buying, get the **6th (2023)** — it includes *Principal Component Analysis (PCA by the SVD)*, which is literally in the GATE DA **ML** syllabus. The 5th (2016) is fine if already owned.
- Layer GATE-format LA questions separately from ~December (PracticePaper, GATE Overflow — GATE CSE/MA/ST maths pools).

## 8. Scope limiters — read before over-preparing

The syllabus is narrower than instinct suggests. Knowing the boundaries is worth weeks.

- **Calculus is single-variable ONLY.** No multivariable calculus, no Lagrange multipliers, no convex optimization, no gradient-descent theory.
- **PDSA has no dynamic programming and no greedy algorithms.** Graphs stop at *traversals and shortest path*. No balanced-tree rotations.
- **ML stops at multi-layer perceptron / feed-forward.** No CNNs, RNNs, transformers. Ignore Keras/TensorFlow architecture material.
- **DBMS has no transactions, concurrency control, or recovery.**
- **AI's uncertainty section is essentially Bayes nets** — conditional independence, variable elimination, sampling. Not a broad AI survey.
- **Programming is Python, not C/Java.**

## 10. DBMS from zero — detailed plan

### Why it's the cheapest subject despite zero exposure

1. **No prerequisites.** ML needs probability + LA. PDSA needs programming maturity. DBMS depends on nothing — start today.
2. **Finite and closed-ended.** The syllabus list is the whole thing. No depth rabbit-hole.
3. **Procedural, not insight-based.** Find candidate keys. Determine highest normal form. Count B+ tree nodes. Trace SQL output. These are drillable to near-100% accuracy — the most reliably convertible marks in the paper.

### The GATE DA trap

Nearly all DBMS resources target **GATE CSE**, which stops at indexing/transactions. GATE DA **adds**:

> data types, data transformation such as normalization, discretization, sampling, compression; data warehouse modelling: schema for multidimensional data models, concept hierarchies, measures.

**No GATE CSE resource covers this.** Source: **Han, Kamber & Pei, *Data Mining: Concepts and Techniques* (3rd ed.), chapters 3–4.** ~1 week. DBMS paid 18 marks in 2026 — do not leave this hole open.

Conversely, GATE CSE material includes **transactions, concurrency, recovery** which are **NOT** in the DA syllabus. Skip entirely — saves ~1 week (~25 videos in the Gate Smashers playlist).

### Book

**Elmasri & Navathe, *Fundamentals of Database Systems* (7th ed.)** — preferred over Korth for DA because Navathe covers data warehousing. **Lookup reference only. Never read front-to-back.**

### 6-week plan

| Wk | Topic | "Done" means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ER model → relational model conversion | Convert any ER diagram to tables; weak entities, cardinalities |
| 2 | Relational algebra + tuple calculus | Fluent with σ, π, ⋈, ÷; translate RA ↔ English |
| 3 | **SQL** | Write *and trace* joins, nested/correlated subqueries, GROUP BY/HAVING, aggregates |
| 4 | Functional dependencies + normal forms | Attribute closure, candidate keys, 1NF→BCNF, lossless/dependency-preserving decomposition |
| 5 | File organization + indexing | B/B+ tree insert/delete by hand, node counts, dense vs sparse |
| 6 | **Data transformation + warehousing** (DA-only) | Star/snowflake/fact constellation, OLAP ops, discretization, sampling |

Weeks 3 and 4 carry the most marks.

### Week 1, day by day

| Day | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Intro, DBMS vs file system, 3-level architecture, data models (~1 hr) |
| 2 | ER model: entities, attributes, relationships, cardinality |
| 3 | ER: weak entities, participation constraints, specialization/generalization |
| 4 | ER → relational schema. **Draw 5 ER diagrams and convert each by hand** |
| 5 | Relational model: keys — super, candidate, primary, foreign; integrity constraints |
| 6 | Relational algebra: σ, π, ×, ∪, −, ⋈ |
| 7 | Consolidate. Redo Day 4 from scratch without notes |

If Day 7's redo feels easy, pace is right.

### Where skimming is safe — and where it is not

Notes replace transcription for the **recall** half of DBMS. They do **not** replace practice for the **procedural** half.

| Safe to skim (pure recall) | Must do on paper (procedural) |
|---|---|
| DBMS vs file system, 3-level architecture, data independence | **ER → relational conversion** |
| Data models, ER diagram notation | **Relational algebra expressions** |
| Types of keys, integrity constraint definitions | **SQL tracing** |
| File organization types, index types (primary/clustered/secondary) | **FDs, attribute closure, candidate keys** |
| Data warehouse schemas, OLAP operation definitions | **Normalization / decomposition** |
| | **B+ tree insert & delete** |

> The right-hand column is where most of the marks are. No notes PDF substitutes for working those by hand.

### ⚠ "Normalization" means TWO different things in this syllabus

The official DBMS line contains both senses, and Gate Smashers only teaches one:

| Sense | Where in syllabus | What it is | Covered by Gate Smashers? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Database normalization** | "...integrity constraints, **normal form**, file organization..." | 1NF / 2NF / 3NF / BCNF, functional dependencies, decomposition | **Yes**, thoroughly |
| **Data normalization** | "...data transformation such as **normalization**, discretization, sampling, compression..." | Rescaling values — min-max, z-score/standardization | **No** — this is data-mining, not DBMS |

If you read the syllabus assuming both mentions are normal forms, you will silently skip a topic. The second one belongs with Han & Kamber ch. 3 (data preprocessing) and overlaps with feature scaling in ML.

**Two gaps in every notes source above:**
1. They all cover **transactions, concurrency, recovery** — **out of the GATE DA syllabus.** Skip those pages.
2. None cover the **DA-only warehousing tail** (data transformation, discretization, sampling, compression). Still need Han & Kamber ch. 3–4.

### What SQL is

A relational database stores data in **tables** (rows = records, columns = fields). **SQL (Structured Query Language)** is how you query and modify them.

Given a `Student` table with columns `RollNo, Name, Dept, Marks`:

```sql
SELECT Name, Marks
FROM Student
WHERE Dept = 'ME' AND Marks > 70;
```

```sql
SELECT Dept, AVG(Marks)
FROM Student
GROUP BY Dept;
```

**Key idea: SQL is *declarative*.** You state **what** you want, not **how** to compute it. No loops, no iteration — you describe the property of the rows you want.

**Link to GATE:** relational algebra is the theory, SQL is the language built on it. `SELECT`/`WHERE`/`JOIN` are σ / π / ⋈. Learn them together.

### SQL practice — order matters

1. **[SQLBolt](https://sqlbolt.com)** — ~2 hrs, syntax from nothing
2. **[SQLZoo](https://sqlzoo.net)** — join and nested-query drills
3. **LeetCode Database, ~25–30 problems, Easy/Medium only** — ~10–15 hrs

**Skip on LeetCode:** anything **Hard**; **window functions** (`RANK`, `DENSE_RANK`, `ROW_NUMBER`, `LAG`/`LEAD`, `OVER PARTITION BY`); **CTEs** (`WITH`), especially recursive; vendor-specific functions. **None are in the GATE DA syllabus.**

**Why LeetCode at all?** GATE asks you to *trace* queries; LeetCode makes you *write* them. Writing thirty queries makes tracing fast and reliable. That's the payoff — it never rehearses the exam format directly.

**LeetCode does NOT help with** relational algebra, tuple calculus, FDs, candidate keys, normal forms, or B+ trees — which are the bulk of the marks. Those come from PYQs.

### Practice source

**GATE CSE has ~20 years of DBMS PYQs**, and ER / relational algebra / SQL / normalization / indexing are **identical** between CSE and DA. Hundreds of exam-format questions vs only 3 DA papers. Topic-tagged on [GATE Overflow](https://gateoverflow.in).

> **Start PYQs from Week 2, not Week 6.** Watch a topic → immediately do 10–15 PYQs on it. Finishing videos feels like competence; solving is a different skill.

### Skip entirely

- Transactions, concurrency control, ACID, serializability, deadlock, recovery/logging
- Query optimization and cost estimation
- Distributed databases, NoSQL

### Success check (end of Week 6)

Cold, without notes, be able to:

1. Take a relation + FDs → find **all** candidate keys
2. State its highest normal form and justify
3. Decompose losslessly
4. Insert a key sequence into a B+ tree of given order and draw the result
5. Read a correlated subquery and say what it returns

## 14. Discipline structure

Context: studying at home, no external schedule, job search running in parallel. The constraint is **structure, not motivation**.

### The leaderboard trap — important

Leaderboards motivate **only once you are actually competitive.** At month 1 of 12, having covered 1.5 of 8 subjects, a full-length mock puts you near the bottom. That reads as failure when it is just an incomplete syllabus.

**Rule:**
- **Months 1–4 (Sep–Dec 2026): topic-wise tests ONLY.** Take a test on a topic right after finishing it, where a good score is achievable and real.
- **From ~Jan 2027, once 4+ subjects are covered: start full-length mocks and leaderboards.** By then the ranking is informative and motivating rather than crushing.

Buying a test series now is right. Taking full mocks now is not.

### What actually creates discipline

1. **Fixed external dates beat question count.** A series with a *scheduled* weekly quiz is worth more than one with 3x the questions and self-paced access. This is why GO Classes' weekly-quiz + daily-homework format suits the problem better than a bigger question bank.
2. **Fixed hours beat total hours.** Same start time daily. The decision of *when* is what erodes without an office or campus.
3. **Public accountability beats private intent.** Post daily progress in a group. Reddit daily threads or the GO Classes Telegram both work.
4. **Interleave interest with duty.** LA and ML are genuinely interesting; DBMS and DSA are the grind. Never schedule two grind blocks back to back.

### The honest framing

The interest in **linear algebra came first**; GATE was retrofitted onto it. That mismatch matters — curiosity sustains effort in a way an instrumental goal does not, so keep a thread of the thing that is actually interesting running at all times rather than treating the year as pure grind.

Also worth holding onto: **GATE DA prep is not an all-or-nothing bet.** Python, data structures, SQL, and machine learning are directly employable skills. Even in the worst case where the rank does not land, the preparation makes you more hireable than the 10 months would have otherwise. That lowers the stakes and, usually, the paralysis with it.
